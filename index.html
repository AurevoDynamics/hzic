§ 1

The same channel that lets ions in fast during charge also governs how they leave during discharge -unless it's shaped to break that link.

THE PROBLEM

Fast charge and high energy density fight for the same volume.

Aqueous zinc storage is intrinsically safe and cheap, but every prior hybrid architecture -including our own -ran into the same geometric wall: a channel network dense enough to admit ions in seconds necessarily occupies most of the electrode. Sparse channels for energy density mean slow charging; dense channels for fast charging mean there's barely any room left for active material.

Our earlier BGC-X design worked around this with three kinetically distinct zones acting in sequence -an EDLC skin for instant response, a pseudocapacitive middle layer, and a slow bulk reservoir. It worked, but it capped Zone C at 300–450 µm and 29–58 Wh/L, because a symmetric channel network still had to serve both directions of ion flow equally.

HZIC-RC asks a different question: what if charge and discharge don't need to share the same ionic pathway at the same speed?

THE ARCHITECTURE

Four advances. One 600 µm reservoir.

Each targets a specific constraint that limited the prior design. Together they take HZIC-RC from 29.0 to a 99.8 Wh/L design target -a 3.44× improvement.

TRANSPORT · #1 IMPACT

Rectified conical microchannels

Channels taper from a 15 µm mouth on the electrolyte side to a 2.5 µm throat on the collector side -access resistance scales inversely with pore radius, so the wide end is easy to enter and the narrow end throttles the exit. Channel volume drops from >55% to <1% of the electrode at equivalent 30 s charge kinetics.

<1% channel volume · ~8× rectification
ELECTROLYTE · #2 IMPACT

Water-in-salt electrolyte

Concentrated Zn(OTf)₂ or ZnSO₄ -salt outnumbers water molecules, which suppresses water oxidation and widens the safe voltage window. Raises practical cell voltage from 1.3 to 1.9 V while dropping electrolyte thickness from 75 to 30 µm.

1.3 → 1.9 V/cell
CATHODE · #3 IMPACT

Oxygen-vacancy α-MnO₂

Calcining Hollandite-structure α-MnO₂ at ~500°C introduces oxygen vacancies that raise electronic conductivity and open extra sites for zinc-ion insertion. Specific capacity rises from 180 to 260 mAh/g, within the literature-validated range.

180 → 260 mAh/g
ANODE · #4 IMPACT

Copper-nanoparticle anode modification

A thin Cu-NP surface treatment gives zinc a far easier surface to nucleate on, so deposits spread evenly instead of concentrating into dendrites. That safely tightens the N/P ratio from 5.9× to 3.1×, reclaiming anode volume.

N/P 5.9× → 3.1×

THE PRINCIPLE

One channel, two timescales.

A conical pore is not symmetric: the access resistance an ion feels entering the wide mouth is roughly six times lower than entering the narrow throat. That single geometric fact is the whole mechanism -it needs no switching, no separate zones, no active control.

PERFORMANCE

Honest numbers. Real constraints.

APPLICATIONS

Built for where safety and endurance decide.

UPS & telecom backup

A ~3-hour sustained discharge from a single reservoir, refilled in under a minute between outages. Zero fire risk removes the facility overhead lithium-ion backup installations require.

Robotics & automation

The wide-mouth channel absorbs a fast top-up during idle periods between duty cycles, while the narrow throat meters out a steady, controlled discharge during operation -no separate charger logic needed.

Micro-mobility

Sub-minute partial charges at any point still meter out a stable, hours-long discharge. 3.8 V nominal remains compatible with existing low-voltage drive electronics.

Industrial IoT buffering

The 10 µm current spreader smooths transient inrush from solenoid actuation and motor starts, while the reservoir underneath absorbs the bulk of the burst through the wide mouth without dead volume.
