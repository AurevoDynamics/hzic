§ 1

The same channel that lets ions in fast during charge also governs how they leave during discharge - unless it's shaped to break that link.

THE PROBLEM

Fast charge and high energy density fight for the same volume.

Aqueous zinc storage is intrinsically safe, cheap, and free of cobalt or lithium - zinc is abundant, non-flammable in water-based electrolytes, and simple to manufacture. But every symmetric-channel electrode design runs into the same geometric wall: a pore network dense enough to admit ions in seconds necessarily occupies most of the electrode volume. Sparse channels favor energy density; dense channels favor fast charge. You can't maximize both through the same symmetric pathway.

A cylindrical-channel network built to fully charge a 600 µm electrode in 30 seconds needs channels occupying more than 55% of that electrode's volume - leaving less than half for the material that actually stores energy.

HZIC-RC asks a different question: what if charge and discharge don't need to share the same ionic pathway at the same speed?

THE ARCHITECTURE

Four supporting decisions around one channel.

The rectified channel geometry is the foundational mechanism - covered next. These four choices are what let the rest of the cell take full advantage of it.

ELECTROLYTE

Water-in-salt formulation

Concentrated Zn(OTf)₂ or ZnSO₄ - salt outnumbers water molecules, which suppresses water oxidation and widens the safe voltage window. Supports a practical 1.9 V/cell operating voltage with >1 V of margin against gas evolution, at 30 µm thickness and 15–22 mS/cm conductivity.

1.9 V/cell · 30 µm
CATHODE

Oxygen-vacancy α-MnO₂

Calcining Hollandite-structure α-MnO₂ at ~500°C introduces oxygen vacancies that raise electronic conductivity and open extra sites for zinc-ion insertion. Specific capacity of 260 mAh/g, within the literature-validated 271–308 mAh/g range for calcined α-MnO₂ at this temperature.

260 mAh/g · 18.7 mg/cm²
ANODE

Copper-nanoparticle modification

A thin Cu-NP surface treatment gives zinc a far easier surface to nucleate on, so deposits spread evenly instead of concentrating into dendrites - roughly 240× the nucleation rate of bare carbon. Supports a tightened N/P ratio of 3.1× without sacrificing dendrite safety.

N/P 3.1× · −15 mV ηnuc
STACK

2S bipolar architecture

Two cells share one internal current collector - fewer interfaces, simpler assembly, and no dead volume from a redundant collector pair. Delivers 3.8 V nominal from a 25 cm² active area and an 884 µm total single-cell stack.

3.8 V · 884 µm total

THE PRINCIPLE

One channel, two timescales.

A conical pore is not symmetric. The access resistance an ion feels entering the wide mouth scales as ρe/4rw - roughly six times lower than entering the narrow throat, ρe/4rn. That single geometric fact is the whole mechanism. It needs no switching, no separate zones, no active control.

Access resistance, wide mouth Raccess,w = ρe / 4rw
Access resistance, narrow throat Raccess,n = (rw/rn) · Raccess,w
Rectification ratio Rrect ≈ (rw/rn) · Kgeom ≈ 8×

The rectification principle isn't zinc-specific. Any electrode where a faradaic, energy-dense material competes with fast-charge channels for volume - sodium-ion layered oxides, other hybrid capacitor chemistries, porous flow-battery electrodes - can use the same asymmetric geometry to decouple the two.

APPLICATIONS

Built for where safety and endurance decide.

UPS & telecom backup

A ~3-hour sustained discharge from a single reservoir, refilled in under a minute between outages. Zero fire risk removes the facility overhead lithium-ion backup installations require.

Robotics & automation

The wide-mouth channel absorbs a fast top-up during idle periods between duty cycles, while the narrow throat meters out a steady, controlled discharge during operation - no separate charger logic needed.

Micro-mobility

Sub-minute partial charges at any point still meter out a stable, hours-long discharge. 3.8 V nominal remains compatible with existing low-voltage drive electronics.

Industrial IoT buffering

The 10 µm current spreader smooths transient inrush from solenoid actuation and motor starts, while the reservoir underneath absorbs the bulk of the burst through the wide mouth without dead volume.
